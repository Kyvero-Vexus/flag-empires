#+SUBTITLE: Book II of the Flag-Empires Chronicle

* Arc 1: Crossing to the Crescent Coasts
** Overview
In this arc, the eastern seaboard of the peninsula transitions from inland caravan governance to maritime dominion. The Admirals' Council assumes control of coastal trade, establishes the first salt courts, and founds the port of Crescent Harbor as the seat of the Crescent Authority. This period marks the beginning of the Second Age of Salt.

- FE-SA-B2-ANN-001: The Admirals' Council convenes in Port Ironwood and ratifies the Charter of the Crescent Authority.
- FE-SA-B2-ANN-002: The first salt courts are established at Crescent Harbor and Saltspire.
- FE-SA-B2-ANN-003: The flagship *Silver Wave* is launched from the Ironwood shipyards.
- FE-SA-B2-ANN-004: The Crescent Authority issues the first Merchant Code.
- FE-SA-B2-ANN-005: Port Ironwood is renamed Crescent Harbor.

* Arc 1: Crossing to the Crescent Coasts
** The Charter of the Crescent Authority

#+begin_quote
[Canon: FE-SA-B2-ANN-001 | Year: SA 1]
In the month of the first tide, the Admirals' Council convened in Port Ironwood. The Charter of the Crescent Authority was ratified by unanimous vote, granting the Admirals semi-hereditary control over coastal trade and maritime law.
#+end_quote

The Charter was a bold departure from the inland governance that had prevailed since the First Age. The Admirals, once mere captains of merchant vessels, now wielded the authority of Seal-Keepers over the crescent-shaped coastline that stretched from Saltspire to the eastern cliffs. The Charter established the Crescent Authority as a semi-hereditary body, with the Admirals' Council serving as its legislative arm. The Seal-Keeper, chosen from among the Admirals, would serve as the executive authority, responsible for enforcing the Charter and overseeing the salt courts.

The Charter was not without its critics. Some inland merchants feared that the Admirals' control over coastal trade would lead to higher prices and reduced access to goods. Others worried that the semi-hereditary nature of the Authority would lead to corruption and nepotism. But the Admirals argued that their control over the coast was necessary to ensure the safety of shipping lanes and the stability of trade. They pointed to the growing threat of piracy and the need for a unified maritime authority to combat it.

** The Founding of the Salt Courts

#+begin_quote
[Canon: FE-SA-B2-ANN-002 | Year: SA 2]
The first salt courts were established at Crescent Harbor and Saltspire. The courts were tasked with adjudicating disputes related to maritime trade, including issues of cargo damage, crew wages, and piracy.
#+end_quote

The salt courts were a novel institution, combining elements of maritime law and traditional jurisprudence. Each court was presided over by a Magistrate, appointed by the Seal-Keeper and confirmed by the Admirals' Council. The courts were funded by a tax on salt, a commodity that was essential to the preservation of food and the maintenance of trade routes. The tax was levied on all salt imported or exported through the crescent coast, and the revenue was used to pay the salaries of the Magistrates and the court staff.

The salt courts quickly became a cornerstone of the Crescent Authority's power. They provided a forum for resolving disputes that might otherwise have led to violence or the breakdown of trade. The courts also served as a check on the power of the Admirals, ensuring that their decisions were subject to judicial review. But the courts were not without their critics. Some merchants complained that the courts were biased in favor of the Admirals and their allies. Others argued that the salt tax was too high and discouraged trade.

** The Launch of the Silver Wave

#+begin_quote
[Canon: FE-SA-B2-ANN-003 | Year: SA 3]
The flagship *Silver Wave* was launched from the Ironwood shipyards. The ship was the largest and most advanced vessel ever built on the crescent coast, and it was intended to serve as the flagship of the Crescent Authority's fleet.
#+end_quote

The *Silver Wave* was a marvel of engineering, with a hull of ironwood and sails of the finest canvas. It was equipped with the latest navigational instruments and armed with a formidable array of cannons. The ship was the brainchild of Captain Lirion, a master shipwright who had spent years designing and building vessels for the Admirals. Lirion was a visionary, and the *Silver Wave* was his magnum opus.

The launch of the *Silver Wave* was a grand affair, attended by the Admirals, the Magistrates, and the leading merchants of the crescent coast. The ship was blessed by the priests of the sea god, and a feast was held in its honor. But the launch was also a moment of tension. Some feared that the *Silver Wave* would be used to enforce the Admirals' will by force, rather than through the courts. Others worried that the ship would be too expensive to maintain and would drain the resources of the Crescent Authority.

** The First Merchant Code

#+begin_quote
[Canon: FE-SA-B2-ANN-004 | Year: SA 4]
The Crescent Authority issued the first Merchant Code. The code established rules for maritime trade, including regulations on cargo, crew wages, and piracy.
#+end_quote

The Merchant Code was a comprehensive set of regulations that governed every aspect of maritime trade. It established rules for the loading and unloading of cargo, the payment of crew wages, and the handling of disputes. The code also included provisions for the punishment of piracy, including the death penalty for those convicted of attacking merchant vessels.

The Merchant Code was a significant step in the consolidation of the Crescent Authority's power. It provided a legal framework for the regulation of trade, and it gave the Authority the tools it needed to enforce its will. But the code was not without its critics. Some merchants argued that the regulations were too strict and would stifle trade. Others worried that the code would be used to favor the Admirals and their allies.

** The Renaming of Port Ironwood

#+begin_quote
[Canon: FE-SA-B2-ANN-005 | Year: SA 5]
Port Ironwood was renamed Crescent Harbor. The renaming was part of a broader effort to rebrand the crescent coast as a unified maritime region under the authority of the Admirals' Council.
#+end_quote

The renaming of Port Ironwood to Crescent Harbor was a symbolic gesture, but it carried significant political weight. The old name had been associated with the inland merchants who had once dominated the region, while the new name reflected the crescent-shaped coastline and the Authority's control over it. The renaming was accompanied by the construction of new docks and warehouses, and the establishment of a customs house to oversee the import and export of goods.

The renaming of Port Ironwood was not without controversy. Some inland merchants refused to acknowledge the new name, continuing to refer to the port by its old designation. Others saw the renaming as a sign of the Admirals' growing power and a threat to their own interests. But the Admirals and their supporters argued that the renaming was necessary to reflect the changing nature of the region and the Authority's role in it.

* Arc 2: Coin, Grain, and Fracture

In the season when the wind turned saltward and the harbors smelled of iron rust, the Crescent Fleet returned not with tribute but with ledgers. The Salt Courts of the Mast-City read them aloud beneath the black canopy, counting copper ingots from the western ports, counting rice from the northern flood plains, counting the silk that passed under tax-toll gates. The numbers grew like vines along the ledger margins, and with them the banners of the tributary lords unfurled in new patterns, each stripe a claim to autonomy.

#+begin_quote
[Canon: FE-SA-B2-ANN-071 | Year: SA 244]

#+end_quote

#+begin_quote

#+end_quote

The wealth of the ports fed the city's hunger for silk, spice, and steel. Merchant galleys from beyond the horizon slipped past the harbor guards with cargoes wrapped in oil-paper. The Seal-Keeper's archive grew heavy with trade charters, each stamped with the crescent seal. Yet the wealth pooled in the harbor basins, leaving the inland plains to starve. Grain caravans grew fewer, their drivers whispering that the Salt Courts preferred copper to corn.

#+begin_quote
[Canon: FE-SA-B2-ANN-093 | Year: SA 251]
A caravan of twelve wagons bound for the capital was stopped at the Dry Seam. The road-wardens demanded three silver bars per wagon. The drivers scattered the grain and fled.
#+end_quote

The Salt Courts responded with the Edict of Even Scales, decreeing that no toll could exceed the weight of the goods carried. The edict was posted on every gate and bridge. But the wording allowed local magistrates to interpret "weight" as they pleased, and the road-wardens found new excuses: rusted axles, missing seals, improper cloth wrappings. The inland lords read the edict as license to raise their own tariffs, each claiming the right to protect their own grain from the capital's hunger.

#+begin_quote
[Canon: FE-SA-B2-EDI-031 | Year: SA 252]

The inland lords sent riders to the capital bearing sealed satchels. The satchels contained neither tribute nor threat, only maps of their own domains with toll stations marked in red ink. The Seal-Keeper read the maps and understood that the salt roads were fragmenting. He sent a lantern-lit envoy to each lord, bearing the black scroll of the Crescent Seal. The scroll promised recognition of their tolls if they would swear allegiance anew. Some signed; others burned the scroll in their council fires.

#+begin_quote

They found the roads lined with toll stations that demanded not coin but loyalty. The Banner-Lord sent couriers ahead with offers of pardon, but the inland lords had already sent their own couriers to neighboring domains, whispering of a new league of salt and grain.

The capital's markets grew thin, and the people whispered that the Salt Courts had grown fat on copper while the people starved. The capital's markets filled with grain, but the Salt Courts' treasury grew empty. The Seal-Keeper ordered the minting of new coins, stamped with the crescent and the red stripe. The merchants accepted them, but the people called them "league silver" and hoarded them.

* Prologue: Salt and Steel

The salt courts of the Second Age had grown fat on levies from every merchant hull that entered the Crescent Ports. Yet prosperity bred its own predators. By the year of the Black Tide, the ledger-keepers found their inkwells running dry even as the coin chests overflowed. Debtors multiplied like barnacles on a ship's hull, and the courts, bound by ancient statute, could no longer grant writs of seizure without unanimous consent from three harbor masters. That unanimity, once a pillar of law, became a weapon in the hands of pirate leagues who held entire districts hostage.

The crisis began not with a cannonade but with a ledger. The Fifth Ledger, kept in the sealed vaults beneath the Iron Bell Tower, recorded every harbor's quota of salt tax and trade tariffs. When the ledger's keeper, a woman named Sariel of the Seventh Ledger, vanished in the night, the courts found their authority unmoored. Without the ledger, no harbor could prove its dues had been paid; without proof, no ship could leave port without risking seizure. The Crescent Ports, once the beating heart of Flagistan commerce, ground to a halt.

* Chapter 1: The Pirate Leagues

The pirate leagues, long dismissed as scourges of the outer seas, saw opportunity in the chaos. They were not a single entity but a constellation of factions: the Red Sails of the northern shoals, the Cutlass Brotherhood of the western reefs, and the Silent Knives who haunted the river deltas. Each league had its own code, but all shared a disdain for the salt courts' levies. When the Fifth Ledger disappeared, they moved to fill the vacuum.

The Red Sails, led by a one-eyed woman known only as the Crimson Widow, blockaded the northern approaches to the Crescent Ports. * Arc 4: The Quiet Partition

In the year SA 427, beneath the vaulted silence of the Salt Courts, the Fifth Banner-Lord signed the Partition Charter. No drums sounded. No banners were lowered. The charter was inked in violet—halfway between mourning and triumph—and distributed across four Mast-Cities: Flagartha, Silvermouth, Ironkeep, and Harborhold. Each received a sealed copy, each swore it had always been the rightful capital.

** The Treaty of Four Gates

The document itself was brief, yet its clauses unraveled the empire in silence. The southern salt roads would belong to Flagistan, the northern grain routes to the Iron League, the eastern spice lanes to the Crescent Compact, and the western ports to the Harbor League.
